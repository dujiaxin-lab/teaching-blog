01 Artificial intelligence6 min read A gentle introduction to machine learning How computers learn from examples, find patterns, and make useful predictions. ↗

Machine learning is a way to teach computers to recognize patterns without writing a rule for every possible situation. Instead of describing every step, we give a model examples and let it adjust its predictions based on what it sees.

Learning from examples

Imagine sorting a collection of photographs into pictures of cats and dogs. A traditional program would need carefully written instructions about ears, tails, and shapes. A machine-learning model can study many labeled examples and learn which combinations of details tend to go together.

The model is not learning in the human sense. It is finding statistical relationships in data. Better examples, thoughtful evaluation, and clear goals all matter more than simply choosing a complicated algorithm.

02 Data science4 min read Start with a question, not a chart A useful data project begins with curiosity and a question that can be investigated. ↗

Charts are compelling, but a chart without a question is just decoration. Before collecting or analyzing data, decide what you want to understand and what evidence could help answer it.

Make the question specific

“How are students doing?” is broad. “Which course topics do students revisit most often before an assessment?” suggests what data to collect and what comparison might be informative. A precise question also makes it easier to notice when the available data cannot support a conclusion.

Good analysis is less about producing more graphics and more about connecting evidence to a decision—while being honest about uncertainty and limitations.

03 Artificial intelligence5 min read What does responsible AI look like? A practical starting point for thinking about fairness, transparency, and human judgment. ↗

Responsible AI is not a single checklist or a property a system can claim for itself. It is an ongoing practice of asking who benefits, who might be harmed, and how people can understand and challenge a system’s decisions.

Keep people in the picture

Begin by describing the system’s intended use and its limits. Examine the data and test results for uneven outcomes. Make it clear when AI is being used, and provide meaningful ways for people to ask questions or request human review.

These steps do not remove every risk, but they make the choices behind a system more visible—and create room to improve them over time.
